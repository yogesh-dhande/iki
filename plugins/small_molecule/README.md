# small_molecule

Iki's small_molecule plugin: compounds, registered by their standard InChIKey,
from SMILES, SDF files, PubChem or ChEMBL, and their batches, each with its
salt form, with RDKit's standardisation and descriptors.

- **`iki/small_molecule.Compound`** (`CPD-000001`): a compound, by its
  standardised structure.
  - Its identity is its standard InChIKey: each compound is registered once
    in a registry (ADR 0115), as one entry with one ID, and registering it
    again, drawn any way, in any project, finds that entry. A project that
    hadn't got it gets its own record of it; one that had finds its record.
  - The entry keeps what follows from the structure: its canonical SMILES
    (required and fixed, like its InChIKey) and standard InChI, and its
    formula, molecular weight, exact mass, charge, heavy atoms, Crippen logP,
    TPSA, hydrogen-bond donors and acceptors, rotatable bonds, rings, aromatic
    rings, fraction Csp3, stereocentres (and how many are undefined), QED and
    Lipinski violations, and its Molfile (its structure laid out in 2D, which
    its exports are written from), which the plugin's actions compute once,
    when it's registered. Every project with a record of it sees them.
  - Its registry facts are the entry's too: its PubChem CID, ChEMBL ID (each
    unique in the registry, and linked to its record there), CAS number, INN
    and stereochemistry. Whoever registers it may set one while it's empty;
    after that only the registry's registrars change it, with a reason.
  - Each project's record keeps its own name, synonyms and description.

- **`iki/small_molecule.CompoundBatch`** (`LOT-000001`): a batch (a lot) of a
  compound, the material in the vial.
  - Its compound is its parent structure, without salts or solvents, as
    registration systems keep them; each batch says its salt form: its salt
    (`hydrochloride`, `sodium`, `mesylate` and 28 others) and how many of it
    for each of the compound, and its solvate (`water` for a hydrate, and 8
    others) and how many.
  - Its formula weight, the weight of the material for each of the compound,
    is computed: weigh doses out by it.
  - It keeps its structure as drawn, salts and all, its name as registered
    (such as aspirin sodium), its purity, amount, supplier, catalogue number,
    supplier's lot and when it arrived.
  - It has no identity: every batch is a batch of its own.

- **`iki/small_molecule.ConformerSet`** (`CNF-000001`): 3D conformers of a
  compound, generated by a run.
  - Its conformers are in an SDF file, with their hydrogens, lowest energy
    first, each with its MMFF94 energy and its energy above the lowest as SD
    fields; and as the models of a PDB file, which its **Conformers** tab shows
    in Iki's structure viewer.
  - It keeps how they were made, how many there are, each one's energy
    (kcal/mol, in the force field they were minimised in: only differences
    mean anything) and how far above the lowest the highest is. A run asked for
    no minimising steps leaves them as embedded, and their energies are single
    points of each as it is, which its method says. A compound
    MMFF94 has no parameters for (selenium, say) gets conformers as embedded,
    with no energies, in the order they were embedded, and its method says
    they weren't minimised.
  - Everything in it is fixed once it's made: generate another set to change
    it.

## Actions

They run on backend workers, in the `chemistry-tools` environment: Python 3.12
with [RDKit](https://www.rdkit.org) 2026.09.2 from PyPI (`rdkit`,
BSD-3-Clause), which asks for 512 MiB. RDKit's wheels carry what it needs
(Boost, and the InChI Trust's InChI library 1.07.3), so the plugin needs no
image of its own, as the protein plugin's Biopython doesn't.

Each action lists its software, by name and the version its environment
installs, so what it makes is recorded as made with it
([ADR 0106](../../docs/adr/0106-how-an-entity-was-made-recorded-by-iki-or-stated-by-its-source.md)):
RDKit 2026.09.2, and the InChI library 1.07.3 for those that identify
compounds. A registered compound is RDKit's standardisation of what it was
given, so it's recorded as made with them, fetched from PubChem or ChEMBL too:
the fetches standardise and identify the record's SMILES as a SMILES line is,
so the identity is Iki's computation, beside the record's origin.

- **`register_compounds`**: registers each line's SMILES (up to 5,000; a name
  may follow, after a space or a tab) as a compound, standardised and
  identified. A compound already registered is found, and so is a line
  that's the same compound as an earlier one: the project gets its record of
  it, or keeps the one it has. With **the parent**, salts and solvents are
  taken off; with **batches**, each line is also a batch of its compound, with
  what was taken off and its formula weight. A line that can't be registered
  fails the run, which names each such line and says why, so nothing is half
  registered; or, with `on_error: skip`, is left out, and the run says so.
- **`compute_descriptors`**: a compound's descriptors, standard InChI and
  Molfile, for many at once. Its trigger, which a project's Admins turn on,
  runs it on each new compound. The Molfile is its SMILES laid out in 2D by
  CoordGen (as RDKit's `SetPreferCoordGen` chooses it), kekulized, with its
  stereocentres wedged and a double bond whose configuration isn't given
  drawn crossed, and an empty title line; V2000, or V3000 when it has more
  than 999 atoms or bonds, which RDKit decides. It's kept only if it reads
  back to the compound's InChIKey. Layouts run in a process of their own, each
  in at most a minute: a structure CoordGen can't lay out in that time or in
  the run's memory (it doesn't finish a cycloalkane ring of 42 carbons, and
  takes a gigabyte over a PEG of 1,000 units), or puts every atom at one
  point (a peptide of 250 residues), is laid out by RDKit's own depictor
  instead. One neither can lay out, or one past the run's 6 minutes of
  layouts, or one whose Molfile would take the run's output over its 2.5 MB,
  gets its descriptors without a Molfile, and the run says so.
- **`compute_formula_weight`**: a batch's formula weight, from its compound's
  structure as it was when the run was launched (ADR 0105). Its triggers run
  it on each new batch, such as one made with its form, and again when its
  salt or solvent changes. A batch whose structure as drawn has a part that's
  neither its compound nor a salt or solvent Iki knows gets none, and the run
  says why.
- **`import_sdf`**: registers each record of an SDF file as a compound,
  standardised and identified as a SMILES line is, and with **batches** a batch
  of each (up to 5,000 records; without, a million, read a record at a time).
  Records and fields are read as BIOVIA's CTfile Formats has them: a field's
  value runs to a blank line, so a line of it starting with `>` (a purity of
  `>99%`) is its value's, and a line starting with `$$$$` ends a record. A
  value's line that reads as the header of a field Iki keeps, as a file
  missing a blank line has, is kept in the value, and the run says so.
  Its name is the SD field chosen, or the record's title. SD fields that are a
  compound's or a batch's fields are kept, matched whatever their case, spaces
  and underscores, and by the names suppliers and PubChem give them (`CAS`,
  `PUBCHEM_COMPOUND_CID`, `Catalog_No`, `Lot`, `Purity`…); the run says which
  others it left out. A salt an SD field gives must agree with the structure's,
  and so must its equivalents, whether it's taken off as the parent's salt or
  registered whole, and whether it's drawn as Iki weighs it or with an isotope
  (lithium-6 is still lithium, so `Salt` Li adds no more). One only the field
  gives (an HCl salt drawn as its free base) is the batch's, even when the
  structure is itself an acid Iki knows as a counterion, drawn alone or with
  its water (benzoic acid with `Salt` Na is sodium benzoate, and acetic acid
  with three waters sodium acetate trihydrate). Its equivalents, when no
  `Salt_eq` field gives them, are as the batch type says: one, or for calcium,
  magnesium or zinc what charge balance says
  ([ADR 0166](../../docs/adr/0166-a-salts-ratio-is-stated-or-follows-from-charge-balance.md)),
  and the run says which. A `Salt` of `N/A`, `none` or `free base` is no salt.
  Without the parent, a salt drawn whole is the compound itself, so a Salt
  field naming it adds nothing to its batch, and the run says so. A record's
  name, synonyms, CAS number, PubChem CID and ChEMBL ID are its material's: a
  salt's, when it's drawn as one, or when its Salt field makes it one, with
  batches or without. So a compound that isn't that material (its parent, or
  the free base drawn) takes none of them, and the run says so; its batch, if
  it has one, takes the name. An amount in a
  field whose name doesn't give its unit (`Amount`, `Mass`, `Quantity`) needs
  one, such as `25 mg`: a bare number there could be a molecular mass, so it's
  left out, saying so; in `Amount_mg` or `Mass_mg` it's in mg. Each
  records the file and record it came from, with the record's SHA-256. A record that can't be imported fails the run, naming each, or with
  `on_error: skip` is left out.
- **`generate_conformers`**: a compound's 3D conformers, as a conformer set.
  RDKit embeds them by distance geometry with ETKDG version 3 (Wang et al.,
  2020), seeded from the run's seed, so a run reproduced exactly makes the same
  ones; drops each whose heavy atoms are within the pruning RMSD of one already
  kept; and minimises the rest in the MMFF94 force field (Halgren, 1996), on
  one thread, so the result doesn't depend on the machine, unless its
  minimisation steps are 0, when they're ranked by their energies as embedded. A compound MMFF94
  has no parameters for is embedded but not minimised, and the run says so; a
  salt (more than one part) fails it: register its parent. It asks for 1 GiB.
- **`fetch_from_pubchem`**: fetches compounds from
  [PubChem](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov) by name, CID or InChIKey (up to
  100, one a line), through PUG REST, and registers each, standardised and
  identified as a SMILES line is, from PubChem's SMILES, with its title as its
  name, its IUPAC name and synonyms (the first 50), its CID, and the first CAS
  Registry Number among its synonyms whose check digit is right. A name PubChem
  has several compounds for takes the first, and the run says which others there
  were. It keeps to PubChem's limit of five requests a second, and when PubChem
  says to slow down (429) or is busy (503) it waits and asks again, up to four
  times.
- **`fetch_from_chembl`**: fetches molecules from
  [ChEMBL](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl) by ChEMBL ID, InChIKey or name (its
  preferred name, or else a synonym), through its web services, and registers
  each from ChEMBL's canonical SMILES, with its preferred name, synonyms and
  ChEMBL ID. A molecule without a structure, such as an antibody, fails the run,
  naming it.

The fetches may reach only their own hosts (`network:`), through the
deployment's egress proxy: `pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov` and `www.ebi.ac.uk`.
Each compound records the record it came from as its origin (ADR 0052): the
source, its CID or ChEMBL ID, its page, when it was fetched, the SHA-256 of
what the service sent, and for ChEMBL the release (`ChEMBL_37`). A compound
registered already is found, its origin noting the record found, and it takes
the record's CID or ChEMBL ID and CAS number if its entry hasn't got them; one
it has with another value is the registrars' to change, so the run fails,
saying so. With **the parent**, a fetched salt is
registered as its parent, which isn't the record fetched, so it doesn't take
the record's IDs or CAS number, and the run says so. Neither fetch lists
software (ADR 0106): what it registers is the service's record, which RDKit
only standardises and identifies, and the services don't say how their
structures were made. A name, ID or InChIKey a service doesn't have fails the
run, naming it, so nothing is half fetched.

- **`similarity_search`**: of the compounds chosen (up to 100,000, such as a
  project's library), those like a query compound, or a query SMILES: the
  Tanimoto similarity of their Morgan fingerprints (radius 2, the environments
  ECFP4 describes, folded into 2,048 bits, as RDKit's fingerprint generator
  makes them) at least the threshold (0.7 unless chosen). They're saved as a
  collection, most similar first, named after the query unless named, whose
  description lists the most similar with their similarity; the run keeps a
  table of every compound's similarity (`similarity.csv`).
- **`substructure_search`**: of the compounds chosen, those with a
  substructure, given as SMARTS (a SMILES is SMARTS too), as RDKit matches it,
  stereochemistry included if asked; saved as a collection, in the order
  given. SMARTS RDKit can't read fails the run, saying why.

Both read their compounds one at a time and keep only each one's ID, name and
SMILES, so a search of 100,000, each with its Molfile, InChI and descriptors,
fits its 1 GiB: 158 MiB at its peak, measured over a 240 MB run input of
100,000 drug-like compounds, where reading them all at once took 2.2 GB.

The searches' results are static collections (`iki/core.Collection`), so they're
what was found when the run ran, kept as they were, to share, export or run
another action on; a collection holds 10,000, and past that the run keeps the
most similar (or the first) and says so. They run over the compounds a run is
given, because a search inside Iki's own (`POST /entities/search`) matches
values, not structures: a dynamic collection can't be a chemical search.

### Standardising

A structure is standardised as RDKit's MolStandardize does it
(`rdMolStandardize.Cleanup`): hydrogens made implicit, bonds to metals broken
(a sodium bonded to an oxygen becomes Na⁺ and O⁻), functional groups drawn in
their usual form (a five-bonded nitro group charge-separated, and so on), and
charges moved to the most acidic sites (reionised). Its SMILES is RDKit's
canonical isomeric SMILES of that, and its identity the standard InChIKey of
its standard InChI. A salt registered as it is is its own compound: sodium
aspirinate is JZLOKWGVGHYBKD-UHFFFAOYSA-M, not aspirin.

Enhanced stereochemistry (a V3000 molfile's or CXSMILES's AND and OR groups)
is something a standard InChI can't hold: it would give the one stereoisomer
drawn, so a mixture of two would be one of them. A group of one stereocentre
(both configurations, or one not known which) says no more than that centre
undefined, so it's registered undefined, as a racemate drawn without wedges
is, and the run says so. For an AND group it says what the two configurations
are: the structure's two enantiomers when nothing else in it is chiral, or two
epimers (diastereomers, such as L-threonine and L-allothreonine) when the rest
of its stereochemistry is defined. It doesn't call either a racemate, which is
an equimolar pair of enantiomers: a group says nothing of proportions. A
group of several centres also says how they relate (cis, not trans), which no
standard InChIKey holds, so the structure fails its line or record, saying
why: draw those centres without wedges and record what's known as the
compound's stereochemistry.

Nor does a standard InChI record an atropisomer's axis, a hindered single
bond's. RDKit reads one (a biaryl's, as in BINOL or sotorasib) from a wedge
beside it, but (R)- and (S)-BINOL have one standard InChIKey, as PubChem's one
record of them shows (CID 11762), and so would be one compound. A structure with an axis
drawn with its configuration fails its line or record, saying which bond it
is: draw the axis without the wedge, which is the compound, whichever
atropisomer it is, and say which in its name. An axis alone in an AND or OR
group says no more than it undefined, so it's registered undefined, as a single
centre in one is, and the run says so.

A cumulene's configuration is the other way about: a standard InChI records it,
but no SMILES does. An allene with two different groups at each end is axially
chiral (penta-2,3-diene's two enantiomers), and three cumulated double bonds
are E or Z, and InChI reads either from a drawing's coordinates (its /t and /b
layers), but RDKit's SMILES has no way to write it. Both enantiomers would be
one SMILES, which a compound's descriptors, depiction and exports are made
from, and which InChI reads as neither. So whenever a structure's InChI has
stereochemistry its SMILES doesn't give back, it fails its line or record,
saying where: draw it without its configuration (in 2D, without wedges), say
which it is in its name, and record what's known as the compound's
stereochemistry.

The parent is what registration systems register: fragments that are salts or
solvents Iki knows are taken off (matched by their structure, neutralised, so
`[Cl-]` and `Cl` are both hydrochloride, and whatever configuration their
stereocentres are drawn with, so sunitinib's (S)-malate is a malate and D-, L-
and meso-tartrate are each a tartrate; a double bond's is kept, which tells
maleate from fumarate, and so is an isotope, so D₂O isn't water, nor ⁶Li⁺
lithium, whose standard atomic weight is mostly ⁷Li's), of what's
left the largest is kept (RDKit's LargestFragmentChooser), and it's
neutralised where it can be (RDKit's Uncharger). So aspirin's sodium salt and aspirin are the same
compound, and its batch says `sodium`, one equivalent. Equivalents count for
each parent: a calcium salt of two aspirins is half a calcium each. A part
that isn't a salt or solvent Iki knows is left out, and the run warns which;
with batches, its batch has no formula weight, since the part is in the
material but what it adds isn't known (hydroxyzine pamoate's would be about
half what it is), and the run says so, with what the drawing weighs for each parent;
two different salts, or solvents, in one structure fail its line, since a
batch records one of each. A structure of nothing but salts and solvents is
what it is (sodium chloride), unless, with its solvents set aside, it's one
compound, drawn once or more: acetic acid drawn with three waters is acetic
acid, its batch a trihydrate. A name given with a structure is that structure's,
so when the parent registered isn't the structure (aspirin from "aspirin
sodium"), its compound isn't given it, nor, from PubChem or ChEMBL, the
record's synonyms or IDs; with batches, the name is its batch's.

A batch's formula weight is its compound's molecular weight, with each
equivalent of its salt and solvent. An acid or a base forms its salt whole (an
amine's hydrochloride adds HCl, 36.46 g/mol); a metal takes the place of its
acid's hydrogens (a sodium salt adds Na less H, 21.98 g/mol; a calcium salt,
for each calcium, Ca less two H). So a calcium salt of an acid with one acid
group is half a calcium for each (calcium acetate, PubChem CID 6116, is 79.08
g/mol for each acetate), and with its equivalents blank, that's what it's
given. One whose compound has two acid groups, or none that Iki counts (a
carboxylic acid, a sulfonic or sulfuric acid, a phosphorus acid's OH, a
tetrazole), could be either, so with its equivalents blank it has no formula
weight, and the run says to give them
([ADR 0166](../../docs/adr/0166-a-salts-ratio-is-stated-or-follows-from-charge-balance.md)). A parent that keeps a charge, such as a
quaternary ammonium, is already the salt's ion, so each unit of its charge is a
hydrogen the salt doesn't add: choline chloride is 139.63 g/mol, choline's
cation and Cl. Equivalents are kept whole (a third for a citrate of three
amines), not rounded. When a structure is drawn whole, salt and all, its
formula weight for each parent must be what the drawing weighs; when it isn't
(its charges don't balance), the run warns.

### Descriptors

From the compound's SMILES, as RDKit 2026.09.2 computes them:

- **Molecular weight**: from IUPAC's standard atomic weights
  (`Descriptors.MolWt`); **exact mass**, monoisotopic (`ExactMolWt`). An atom
  drawn as a particular isotope counts at that isotope's mass in both, and the
  **formula** writes it so (`CalcMolFormula` with separate isotopes): heavy
  water is D2O, and carbon-13 methanol [13C]H4O.
- **Crippen logP**: Wildman and Crippen's atom contributions (1999).
- **TPSA**: Ertl's topological polar surface area (2000), of its nitrogens and
  oxygens, in Å².
- **H-bond donors and acceptors**: RDKit's own definitions
  (`CalcNumHBD`, `CalcNumHBA`), which differ from Lipinski's.
- **Lipinski violations**: of the rule of five, with its original counts:
  molecular weight over 500, logP over 5, more than 5 NH and OH, more than 10
  N and O.
- **Rotatable bonds**: RDKit's default (strict) definition, which leaves out
  the partly double bonds of amides, esters and their kin, bonds to
  triple-bonded atoms, and bonds to CF3, CCl3, CBr3 and tert-butyl groups (so
  N-methylacetamide has none). **Rings**: RDKit's count (`CalcNumRings`), of
  its symmetrised smallest set of smallest rings, which for a symmetric cage
  counts each ring it could have chosen: 6 for cubane, whose smallest set (its
  independent cycles) has 5.
- **Stereocentres**: tetrahedral centres, by RDKit's current stereo
  perception, and how many the structure leaves undefined.
- **QED**: Bickerton's quantitative estimate of drug-likeness (2012), with its
  mean weights.

A compound whose InChIKey isn't the one its SMILES gives, as one made by hand
through Iki's form or API might be, fails its own run, naming both keys;
register its structure with `register_compounds` instead.

## Exports

A compound downloads as a Molfile (`.mol`) or an SDF file from its record page,
and many at once as one SDF file from the records table (ADR 0229). Iki writes
them itself, from each compound's stored Molfile at the revision exported, with
its name as the Molfile's title and, in an SDF, its fields as the record's
data: the file is pinned to that revision, each download is in the audit log,
and no worker runs. That replaces the plugin's `export_sdf` action, which laid
compounds out again on a worker for each export.

## Its structure, drawn

A compound's page has a **Structure** tab, the plugin's `depiction` component:
its 2D structure, drawn in the browser by [RDKit.js](https://rdkitjs.com) from
its SMILES, with its stereocentres labelled (R and S), and its SMILES under it
to copy. RDKit.js is RDKit's MinimalLib compiled to WebAssembly, at the same
release (2026.09.2) as the plugin's actions run, so the drawing is of the
structure RDKit registered, perceived the same way.

It's drawn in the browser rather than stored as a picture. A component runs in
a sandboxed frame that may compile WebAssembly but reaches nothing (ADR 0084),
so RDKit.js, bundled into its module, can draw any compound at once, at any
size, from the SMILES Iki already has. An SVG drawn by a run and kept in a file
field would cost a file for every compound, and a new compound would show
nothing until a run had drawn it. Nor could the page show it: Iki shows file
fields as downloads, and a component can't read files. A drawing kept in a
text field would show as its markup.

The module (`components/depiction.js`, 3.2 MiB) is built by `web/build.js`
from the shared `@ikisci/science/chemical-depiction` element, its pinned
RDKit.js (2026.9.2, BSD-3-Clause), and the field adapter in `web/src/depiction.js`. It holds RDKit.js's
loader, then its WebAssembly, gzipped and in base64, then the component. The
frame unpacks the WebAssembly with the browser's `DecompressionStream` and
hands it to RDKit.js, so RDKit.js fetches nothing. Rebuild it with
`pnpm --filter @ikisci/small-molecule-web bundle`; `pnpm test` (in
`make check`) fails if the committed module isn't what they build.

One change is made to RDKit.js, because the frame's policy rightly allows no
`eval`. The Emscripten release RDKit.js was built with writes the JavaScript
side of each function embind binds as source and compiles it with
`new Function`, and emval does the same for its method callers. The policy
refuses that in each browser, as the plugin's test found. `web/packages/science/tools/rdkit-no-eval.mjs`
swaps those two places for closures that do what their source did, much as
Emscripten itself does without dynamic code: emval takes a closure under
`-sDYNAMIC_EXECUTION=0`, and newer releases build embind's invokers ahead of
time. Each place is found by its exact text, so a release that changes them
fails the build rather than slipping through, and the build checks that
nothing in the module evaluates code. The WebAssembly is RDKit's, unchanged.
The drawing is an SVG shown as an image, so nothing in it runs.

### Drawing a structure to register it

Not yet. Iki's components can show what they're given and tell the page what a
person picked, but they can't change anything, start runs or call Iki's API
(ADR 0084), so a drawing tool in one could only draw, not register. Ketcher, the
structure editor (Apache-2.0), is also larger than a component module may be:
`ketcher-standalone` 3.18.0 is 110 MB unpacked, with its Indigo WebAssembly,
against a module's 10 MiB. Until components can hand the page values to use,
draw in a structure editor and paste its SMILES into `register_compounds`, or
export an SDF file for `import_sdf`.

## Developing it

From the repository root:

```sh
uv run iki plugin validate plugins/small_molecule
cd plugins && uv run iki plugin makemigrations small_molecule   # after changing a manifest
iki plugin test plugins/small_molecule
```

Its environment's packages are pinned, with their hashes, in
`requirements.txt`, compiled from `requirements.in` as it says. Pin the same
releases in the repository's dev dependencies, where
`server/tests/test_small_molecule_actions.py` runs the actions: `make
check-deps` checks they match, and that each is at least 3 days old. The
`software` versions are RDKit's own (`rdkit.__version__`, 2026.09.2, which
PyPI calls 2026.9.2) and the InChI library's (`rdkit.Chem.inchi.GetInchiVersion()`).

`server/tests/test_first_party_plugins.py` checks that it's valid, that its
migrations match its manifests, and that it migrates forwards and back. The
dev cluster's end-to-end check (`deploy/dev/sso.py`) registers aspirin and its
sodium salt, checks its InChIKey, batches and descriptors, and that aspirin
drawn another way is the same record, and fetches caffeine from PubChem through the
egress proxy, checking that it reached PubChem alone, and finds aspirin's
carboxylic acid with a substructure search, saved as a collection of it.

`server/tests/test_small_molecule_depiction.py` loads the depiction in Chromium,
Firefox and WebKit, in a frame with the component frame's policy and Iki's
hardening script. It checks that aspirin is drawn, that nothing is refused or
fetched beyond the frame and its scripts, and that a SMILES RDKit can't read
says so.

The fetches' tests (in `server/tests/test_small_molecule_actions.py`) answer
them from fakes of PubChem and ChEMBL, shaped as the services answered when
they were written: PUG REST's `PropertyTable` and `InformationList`, and the
ChEMBL web services' molecule records.
