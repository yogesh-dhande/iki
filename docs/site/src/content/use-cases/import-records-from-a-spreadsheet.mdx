## In short

The institute's protein production core has purified three KRAS pathway proteins and sends eight
barcoded tubes with a manifest, a CSV file its LIMS exported. The lab imports the manifest as
**Tube** records of the inventory plugin, each holding one of the proteins the project has
registered.

Any type whose records people can make can be imported this way, with no plugin of its own: a
sheet's rows become records of the type you choose, its columns their fields. Iki checks every row
before it writes any, and writes them all in one run, or none. Each record keeps the workbook,
sheet and row it came from.

The core writes numbers with a decimal comma and dates day first, and its columns are separated by
semicolons. Row 8 repeats row 7's barcode, and the project hasn't registered PDEδ yet.

<Step n={1} id="workbook" title="Choose the workbook and what its rows are" role="Lab member">

On the project's **Entities** tab choose **Import**, or on the **Tubes** page choose **Import**,
then the project. Choose **Its rows are: Tubes**, then upload the manifest, or choose one already
in the project's files.

Iki reads an Excel workbook (`.xlsx`) or a CSV file of up to 100 MiB, with up to a million rows and
1,000 columns a sheet. It reads the values the workbook saved, never its formulas or macros. A
workbook with a password, an Excel 97–2003 workbook (`.xls`), or an `.xlsb`, `.ods` or Numbers
file needs saving as `.xlsx` first. A CSV file can be UTF-8, UTF-16 with its byte order mark, or
Windows-1252 as Excel writes it, separated by commas, semicolons, tabs or bars: Iki finds which.

The workbook stays in the project's **Files**, and each record the import writes names it. Until
you import its rows, the import is yours: nobody else sees it.

</Step>

<Step n={2} id="rows" title="Choose the sheet and its rows" role="Lab member">

Iki starts with the first visible sheet, its first row with anything in it as the header, and
every row after it. Change the **Sheet**, the **Header row** (0 if it has none), the **First row**,
the **Last row** (empty for the sheet's last), and **Rows to leave out**, such as notes at the
bottom of a sheet (`5-7, 12`). The page shows the sheet's first rows as you choose.

For the manifest, the header is row 1 and the rows are 2 to 9.

</Step>

<Step n={3} id="columns" title="Map its columns to fields" role="Lab member">

Iki maps a column to the field its header names, ignoring case, punctuation and a unit in brackets,
or to the one field it abbreviates. Here **Barcode** maps to Barcode, **Prepared** to Prepared, and
**Conc. (ug/mL)** to Concentration, in µg/mL. Map **Label** to **Name** and **Protein** to
**Contents** yourself. A column mapped to nothing isn't imported.

Each column is read as its field:

- A **quantity** is in the column's unit, or the cell's own (`2,1 mg/mL`), and kept in the field's:
  the tubes' concentrations are kept in mg/mL, so 980 µg/mL is 0.98 mg/mL.
- A **reference** names a record by its ID, its HRID or its name, in any case, among the records
  you can use in the project: **KRAS G12C (1-169)** is the project's protein of that name. A name
  several records have is a problem that names them.
- A **list** is split by `;`, or the separator you choose.
- A **choice** matches its values in any case, and a **true or false** field reads `yes`, `no`, `x`
  and the like. The column can say what its own spellings mean, such as `circ.` for circular.
- A **date** is ISO 8601 (`2026-11-03`), written out (`3 Nov 2026`), or numbers in the sheet's
  order. A date and time without a time zone needs the column's.

Computed fields, state fields, files and structured fields can't be mapped, and the field list
says why. Add files to the records once they're imported.

Choose **Save this mapping** to name it for the project: the next sheet whose headers include all
of its columns is mapped by it, wherever the columns are.

</Step>

<Step n={4} id="locale" title="Say how its numbers and dates are written" role="Lab member">

Iki never guesses how a sheet writes numbers and dates. Until you say, a cell that reads two ways
is a problem of its own: `1.250` is 1.25 with a decimal point and 1 250 with a decimal comma, and
`03.11.2026` is 3 November day first and 11 March month first. A cell that reads only one way is
read so: `2,1` and `14.11.2026`. Cells a workbook stores as numbers or dates read one way only.

For the core's manifest, choose **Decimal mark: A decimal comma** and **Dates written as numbers:
Day first**. The page shows a few of each column's cells as they now read: `1 250 ug/mL =
1.25 mg/mL`, and `2026-11-03`. Read with a decimal point, the KRAS G12C tubes would have been a
thousand times too dilute.

Where a date column lost its format and shows plain numbers such as `46023`, choose **Plain numbers
in date columns are Excel serial dates**. The run that imports the rows keeps how they were read.

</Step>

<Step n={5} id="preview" title="Preview every row" role="Lab member">

The preview checks every row as it would be written: each cell as its field, each record against
the type's rules, and each unique value, such as a tube's barcode, against the project and the
rows before it. It counts what importing each row would do:

| What it would do                 | What that means                                                                     |
| -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **New record**                   | A record of the project's own.                                                      |
| **New registry entry**           | A record of a new entry, for a type registered by its identity, such as a sequence. |
| **Registered already**           | A record of an entry another project registered.                                    |
| **In the project already**       | The project's record of that entry, kept as it is.                                  |
| **Same as an earlier row**       | The same entry as an earlier row: one record.                                       |
| **Has problems**                 | What's wrong, by its column, such as a reference that names no record.              |

Rows that a plugin's rule would hold for review are counted too: they're imported, and held.

For the manifest, 6 rows are new records and 2 have problems, listed first:

- Row 8: **Barcode “PPC2611-0423” is row 7’s too: no two share it.**
- Row 9: **Contents** names no record by its ID, HRID or name: the project has no PDEδ yet.

Changing anything, such as the mapping, starts the preview afresh.

</Step>

<Step n={6} id="import" title="Import the rows" role="Lab member">

Rows with problems stop the import unless you choose **Leave out the 2 rows with problems**. Give a
**Why**, such as "Core delivery PPC-2611, KRAS and SOS1 tubes", for the run and the audit trail,
and choose **Import 6 rows**.

One run of **Import a spreadsheet** writes the rows, in row order, so the tubes' HRIDs follow the
manifest's order. It checks every row again first, as the project is then, and writes all of them
or none. If someone used one of the barcodes since the preview, nothing is imported, and the
import says which row and column.

</Step>

<Step n={7} id="check" title="Check what it imported" role="Lab member">

Choose **Show the imported records** for the tubes the run wrote. Each tube's page shows, under
**Imported from**, the file, its row (`row 4`; for a workbook, its sheet too, such as `Samples,
row 14`) and the run. Its history names the run, and through it you, and any agent acting for
you. The rows the run imported are kept as a file of the project too,
`ppc-manifest-2611 (rows imported).ndjson`, and the audit trail records the import, with the
workbook, sheet and how its numbers and dates were read.

</Step>

## Import more rows of the same sheet later

The core confirms that row 8 is PPC2611-0424 and sends a corrected manifest, and PDEδ is
registered in the project. Import the corrected file with **First row: 8**. The import doesn't
update records it finds: the six tubes are the project's already, so their rows would be problems,
such as **Barcode “PPC2611-0418” is TUB-000231’s already: no two share it.**

An import you leave for a fortnight is discarded, and the workbook stays in the project. A
committed import's rows are cleared a fortnight after, and its records, run and files stay.

## When a plugin's import fits better

The spreadsheet import writes one record a row, of one type, as the sheet's columns say. Ask your
plugin's author for an import action when:

- the file is an instrument's or a vendor's own layout, such as values above the table that every
  record needs
- one row becomes several records that refer to each other, such as a sample and its library
- importing needs science, such as standardising a structure or checking a RIN against a
  threshold
- the same file comes again, revised, and should update the records it made, rather than stop at
  each one the project has
- it runs as a step of a workflow

[Import records from a file](/guides/write-a-plugin/import-from-a-file/) shows how one is written.

## Other ways data comes into Iki

- **One record at a time**: **New** on a type's page or in a project.
- **Files**: upload them on a project's **Files** tab, or wherever a file field or an action asks
  for one. Iki checks each upload against its SHA-256 before it's a file, and its stored bytes never
  change. When its data was acquired is read from the file where its format records it, such as an
  AB1 trace, an FCS file, an mzML run, a JCAMP-DX spectrum, an image's EXIF or an OME-TIFF, or a
  SAM or BAM header; otherwise it's what you say when you upload it.
- **Files where they already are**: a file in a bucket location your admins approved can be
  registered in place instead of uploaded, through the REST API or the Python SDK. Its bucket must
  keep versions: Iki pins the version it registers, so a later write to the same name can't change
  it, and reads it once to learn its SHA-256. Its page says **Registered where it is**. Keep such
  buckets under a retention policy: Iki can't stop the bucket's owner from deleting a version.
- **Plugins' imports**: actions that read a file or fetch from a public database, run from the
  project's **New** menu, **Run**. Each record they make says where it came from, and one already
  registered is found again rather than made twice.
- **Another Iki deployment**: a bundle, with each record's history, imported in a project's
  **Settings** by an Org Admin who is also the project's Admin. Iki shows what importing it would
  do before anything changes.
- **Scripts and agents**: the [Python SDK](/reference/python-sdk/#spreadsheet-imports) imports a
  spreadsheet the same way, and uploads and registers files. An agent connected through the
  [MCP server](/reference/python-sdk/#mcp-server) makes records and runs actions, such as a
  plugin's import, as the person it acts for.
