## In short

The example releases a lot of sodium chloride (PubChem CID 5234) against two criteria from the
USP sodium chloride monograph, official on 1 December 2015: an assay of 99.0–100.5% NaCl on the
dried basis by potentiometric titration (USP &lt;541&gt;), and loss on drying of at most 0.5% (USP
&lt;731&gt;). The specification is a teaching excerpt of the monograph, and
the lot, its results and its investigation are synthetic.

The deployment needs the QC release and lots plugins. An admin chooses the **Release testing
review** setting: **Research**, where one QC reviewer signs, or **Regulated QC**, where an analyst,
an independent reviewer and QA each sign. A release case keeps the flow it was opened under. The
material is registered, and the lot is a **Lot** record with its **Lot number**, in
**Quarantined**.

<Step n={1} id="specification" title="Publish the specification" role="Release testing QA">

Create one **Specification schedule** for the material, and a **Specification version** in it with
an effective date, its source and one criterion for each attribute. Each criterion names its method
and unit, and has an **Inclusive lower limit**, an **Inclusive upper limit**, or both.

      <tr><td>Loss on drying</td><td>USP &lt;731&gt; Loss on Drying</td><td>%</td><td></td><td>0.5</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

Sign the version **Approved for publication**, then run **Publish an approved specification
version** on the schedule. A later version must start on a future date after the one before it,
and every earlier version is kept.

</Step>

<Step n={2} id="open" title="Open release testing" role="QC analyst">

On the lot's **QC release** tab, run **Open lot release testing** with the material's schedule and
the **Testing date**. Iki picks the approved version in force on that date, and the new **Lot
release case** records the lot number and the revisions of the lot, schedule and version it read.
A lot has one case, which keeps every attempt.

Opening is refused if the lot has no lot number, the testing date is in the future, or no approved
version was in force on that date.

</Step>

<Step n={3} id="test" title="Record the results" role="QC analyst">

For each criterion, run **Record a release test** with its method, unit and **Observed value**, and
attach the **Laboratory report**. The method and unit must be the specification's. Limits are
inclusive, so 99.0 passes a lower limit of 99.0. A result can instead come from a signed plate
assay result of the same lot; Regulated QC takes only a QA-released one.

The assay reads **98.7**, below the lower limit. Recording it opens an **Out-of-specification
investigation** for that attempt.

</Step>

<Step n={4} id="investigate" title="Investigate the result" role="QC analyst, QC reviewer">

In the investigation, record the **Resolution**, **confirmed** or **invalidated**, and the
**Findings**. Invalidating a result needs **Evidence** of the assignable cause. Here the findings
are "the report ID belongs to a different specimen", and the result is invalidated.

In Research the QC reviewer signs it **Signed off**, with the meaning Review. In Regulated QC the
analyst signs **Submitted** (Authorship), a QC reviewer who didn't record the result or create,
submit or edit the investigation signs **Independently reviewed** (Review), and release testing QA,
someone else again, signs **QA released** (Approval).

A confirmed result out of specification means the lot can't have a passing certificate. QA can put
the lot **On hold** or reject it.

</Step>

<Step n={5} id="repeat" title="Repeat the test" role="QC analyst">

Run **Record a release test** again for the assay, and choose the attempt it repeats and that
attempt's signed investigation. The repeat reads **99.9**, and loss on drying reads **0.2**: both
pass. Once a criterion has a passing attempt, no further attempt is accepted for it.

The original 98.7 stays in the case, with its investigation.

</Step>

<Step n={6} id="certificate" title="Prepare and sign the certificate" role="QC analyst, QC reviewer">

Run **Prepare a certificate of analysis** on the case with every attempt and each signed
investigation. Iki refuses if an attempt or an investigation is left out, an investigation isn't
signed, a result out of specification was confirmed, or a criterion has no passing attempt. The
certificate records the testing date, the lot, the specification version and its criteria, every
attempt with its value, limits, outcome and report, and each investigation's resolution, findings
and evidence. Preparing it seals the case.

</Step>

<Step n={7} id="release" title="Release the lot" role="Lot QA">

Attach the certificate under the lot's **Certificates of analysis**, with any supplier
certificates, and set its **Disposition** to **Released**. The signature means Approval, labelled
QA release. A lot can't be released without a certificate attached, by the person who made,
recorded or edited a lot made in the lab, or through an app or agent.

A released lot is locked. Sending it back to **Quarantined** needs a reason.

</Step>

## What Iki does not decide

Iki checks each result against the specification's limits, holds a result out of specification,
and records the investigation, the retest and every signature. Whether the investigation found the
real cause, whether a retest is justified and whether the lot is fit to release are QA's decisions
under the lab's procedures.
