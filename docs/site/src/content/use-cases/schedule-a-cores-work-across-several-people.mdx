## In short

In a core with several people, one of them usually decides what runs when. Here Sam Adeyemi
coordinates a sequencing core with a NextSeq 2000 for short reads and a GridION for long reads,
and two analysts run them. Deciding the order is Sam's judgement, made by hand. Iki gives him what
he needs to make it, and keeps the result straight:

- The core's Requests queue shows each request with the project that asked, and filters by it.
- Each request has the date its project needs it by, and the core's own fields: its **priority**,
  Routine or Urgent, which the core decides, its **scheduled date** and its **analyst**.
- Each instrument has a calendar of bookings, and Iki refuses two bookings of one instrument that
  overlap, or a booking of an instrument that's out of service.

Iki doesn't order the queue for him, or hold a policy of which project goes first.

## What Iki does and doesn't do

- It shows the asking project as a column of the core's queue, and filters by it, by status, by
  priority, and by any other field of the request.
- It sorts the queue by one field, then by up to three **Then by** sorts for what that ties, such
  as priority with Urgent first, then needed-by with the soonest first. A choice such as priority
  sorts in the order the request type lists its values, not alphabetically. A saved search keeps
  the order.
- It puts a request on its analyst's My work while it's Accepted or In progress, soonest needed
  first. An Accepted request with no analyst is open to every Analyst of the core, who can take it.
- It refuses a booking that overlaps another of the same instrument, unless one is cancelled, and
  a booking of an instrument that isn't in service.
- A booking can be for someone other than whoever made it, and can name the request it's for. It
  shows on that person's home, and the request's lineage lists it. Cancelled bookings are kept,
  and calendars leave them out unless asked to show them.
- It doesn't decide the order. Priority is a field the core sets, and the order is the
  coordinator's.

## Variations

- Rank the projects a core serves, and sort the queue by rank first. Iki holds no ranking policy of
  its own; a lab builds one from general pieces. It adds a request type that extends Plain request
  with a field of the core's own, such as the asking project's rank, and keeps its ranking as
  records in the core's project. A trigger that runs when another project sends a request runs an
  action, as a service account an Admin chooses and on that Admin's authority, that reads which
  project asked and sets the rank. When the ranking changes, a coordinator selects the open
  requests and runs the action again. The request priority example, in
  `examples/plugins/request_priority/` of Iki's repository, is a working template.
- Keep the core's notes on each run private, in a work item: see
  [Keep a core's working records private](/use-cases/keep-a-cores-working-records-private/).
- Release results only after an independent review and QA: use the
  [QC request](/reference/plugins/requests/#QCRequest) and
  [QC deliverable](/reference/plugins/requests/#QCDeliverable).
