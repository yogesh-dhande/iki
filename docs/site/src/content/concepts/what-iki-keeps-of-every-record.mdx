## In short

Every record in Iki, of whatever type and in whatever process, keeps the same history. A use case
doesn't have to set any of this up:

- **Every change is a revision**, numbered, with who made it, through what, when, which fields it
  changed, and why if they said.
- **A reason, wherever the process asks for one.** A declined or withdrawn request, a cancelled
  work item and an instrument taken out of service each keep the reason they were given.
- **The person behind an agent.** A change an AI agent or an app makes names both the agent and
  the person whose access it acted with.
- **Signatures bound to what they sign**, which anyone who can read the record can check again.
- **Nothing is deleted or rewritten.** A correction adds to the history; a record is archived, not
  deleted.
- **An audit trail to take away**, as CSV.

## How it works

### Every change is a revision

A revision says what kind of change it was: the record created, edited, set to a state, archived,
restored, or changed by a plugin's upgrade. It keeps the fields it changed, the person who made it,
how the change came (the web app, the API, an agent through MCP, the CLI, an action's run, or Iki
itself during an upgrade), the time, and the reason when one was given. Iki can show the record as
it was at any revision, and for each value, the revision that set it.

Revisions are append-only for every role, Iki's own database role included. More in
[Records and revisions](/concepts/records-and-revisions/).

### A reason where the process asks for one

A plugin's state can require a reason from whoever sets it, and Iki refuses the change without
one. The reason is kept on the revision, up to 2,000 characters. In Iki's own plugins:

- a core declines a request, and a project withdraws its own, with a reason
  ([Requests](/reference/plugins/requests/#PlainRequest));
- an analyst cancels a work item with a reason
  ([Work item](/reference/plugins/requests/#WorkItem));
- whoever finds an instrument broken sets it out of service, saying why
  ([Instrument](/reference/plugins/equipment/#Instrument)).

A correction of a locked record always takes a reason: see [Corrections](/concepts/corrections/).

### The agent and the person it acted for

A person can let an agent, such as an AI assistant through Iki's MCP server, act with their access.
Each revision the agent makes names the person and the agent: its client, the person's grant it
acted under, and the software and model it said it is. A run's revisions name whoever launched it.
Agents, apps and service accounts never sign: only a person, signing in again, does.

### Signatures

A state can take electronic signatures, each with a meaning such as review or approval, and a
statement its signer attests to. Each signature names its signer, the time, the reason and the
revision it signs, with a hash of the record's content then, and the deployment signs it with its
own key. A signature is never deleted: one that lapses or is withdrawn stays, marked so. More in
[States, locks and signatures](/concepts/states-locks-and-signatures/).

### What crosses between projects

When a request is sent, each side's audit log records it: sent in the project that asked, received
in the core's. Receiving its tubes, and releasing each result back, are recorded too. A record a
request brings into a project goes no further.

### The audit trail

A record's page shows its revisions, the values at each, and where each value came from. Anyone
who can read the record can download its audit trail as CSV, from the web app or the REST API.
Webhooks and the organization's audit log hear of each revision without its values, which a
receiver reads through the API with its own access.
