## In short

In Iki a correction adds to a record's history and never rewrites it. The corrected value is a new
revision, with who made it and why, and the value it replaces stays at the revision that recorded
it, as do any signatures given on it.

How a value is corrected depends on what's happened to the record since. An unreviewed value is
edited. A value a state has locked, such as an approved result, is corrected by returning the
record to an earlier state with a reason, which sends it back through review. An amount, a
location and a registry fact each have a correction of their own.

## How it works

### Editing a value

A value that no state locks is corrected by editing it. The edit is a revision of kind
**Edited**, and the record's history keeps the value before it. A reason is optional, and kept when
given; a field can ask for one when it's cleared, as the Inventory plugin's **On hold** flag on a
tube does.

A record made in error is archived, saying why, rather than deleted: it keeps its ID, its history
and everything that refers to it.

### Correcting a locked record

A locked record is corrected by first setting it back to a state that doesn't lock, as its plugin
allows, with a reason, and then editing it. In the Requests plugin, an analyst, reviewer or QA
returns a submitted or reviewed QC deliverable to Recorded, saying why; in the Samples plugin, a
reviewer returns a sample whose results are recorded to testing, which takes a signature and a
reason. The analyst then corrects the values and submits the record again.

The record goes through its review again. The signatures given before stay with the revisions
they signed, and still verify: they attest to what the record said then, not to the corrected
value. A guard can read who edited the record since it returned, so that whoever corrected a
result doesn't review it.

A locking state that isn't signed for can instead name where a correction returns the record. When
the record is in that state, editing a value is the correction:

1. The form says where saving will take the record, such as "Saving these changes will return
   Status from Approved to In testing", and asks why.
2. Iki checks that the editor may edit the record and may set it to that state, its lab roles and
   guards included.
3. The return and the edit are saved together, as two revisions in that order, each with the
   editor and the reason. If either is refused, neither is saved.

A state that takes signatures can't name such a return, so a signed record is always returned on
its own first, by someone its plugin allows.

### Corrections with histories of their own

- **An amount.** How much there is of a lot or a tube is adjusted to what it was found to be, with a
  required reason. The adjustment is a new movement in its ledger; earlier movements never change.
- **A location.** Moving a record that was put in the wrong place is an event in its location
  history, with a required reason.
- **A registry fact.** A fact shared by every project holding an entry is changed by one of the
  registry's registrars, with a reason, and every holding project is told. A fixed fact isn't
  changed at all: its entry is superseded with one that's right, and each project moves its record,
  with a reason.
- **A hold.** A record a rule held, such as a result out of its specification, stays held until
  someone with the rule's lab role, other than whoever saved it, reviews the hold and says why it
  may stand.
- **A signature.** Its signer can withdraw it, with a reason, until the state it's for is set.

## Worked example

The QC deliverable `QCD-000012`, a SEC purity result of 97.8%, was reviewed on 18 November 2026.
On 19 November, Priya Natarajan, its analyst, finds that the chromatography report gives 97.3%:
she typed it wrongly. The deliverable is locked by its Review (Reviewed), so she can't edit it as
it is.

Priya sets its Review back to Recorded, with the reason "Transcription error: the chromatography
report gives 97.3%." The deliverable is unlocked, and she corrects the purity:

| Revision | Kind          | Change                          | By              | Reason                                                       |
| -------- | ------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 6        | State changed | Review: Submitted to Reviewed   | Tomás Herrera   |                                                              |
| 7        | State changed | Review: Reviewed to Recorded    | Priya Natarajan | Transcription error: the chromatography report gives 97.3%. |
| 8        | Edited        | Purity: 97.8% to 97.3%          | Priya Natarajan | Transcription error: the chromatography report gives 97.3%. |

Tomás's review signature, carried by revision 6, stays where it is, bound to the values he reviewed,
with 97.8%; its check still passes. Priya submits the deliverable again, signing for authorship,
and Tomás reviews it again. Had Tomás made the correction himself, the plugin's guard on Reviewed
would refuse his review: he edited it since it was last recorded, so someone else reviews it.

The same week, a recount of the Tris lot `RMG-000031` finds 985.0 g where its ledger says 987.9 g.
Marta Okafor adjusts it to 985,000 mg with the reason "Recount on 21 Nov 2026: spill when weighing
on 20 Nov". The ledger keeps the earlier movements, and the adjustment of −2,900 mg after them.

## The rules in full

### Corrections of locked records

- A return to an earlier state is a state change like any other: it takes one of that state's lab
  roles, passes its guards, and keeps the reason, which the state can require.
- A correction made by editing is offered only while every state field locking the record is in a state that names
  a return. The API says so in the record's `can.correct`.
- A correction needs a reason, up to 2,000 characters. Without one, the save is refused.
- A state that takes signatures can't name a return. The return's destination is a live state that
  doesn't lock and isn't signed for. The editor must
  hold one of its lab roles, and its guards must pass. If several state fields return, they return
  in the order the type declares them, and each guard sees the returns before it.
- The return and the edit are saved in one transaction, under the record's lock, against the
  revision the editor read. A stale revision, a refused guard or a value that breaks a rule saves
  nothing.
- Submitting a value equal to the one stored changes nothing, and the record stays in its state.
- Runs and bulk imports never correct a locked record: their changes to it are refused.
- A result released to the project that requested it is final: its values and states can't change.
- Archiving, restoring and moving to another registry entry need an unlocked record.

### What a correction keeps

- Every value a record has had is in its history, at the revision that set it, with who, how, when
  and why.
- A signature stays bound to the revision it signed. It's never moved to the corrected revision, and
  a corrected record needs the signatures its states ask for again.
- A record's audit trail lists the return and the edit as separate revisions, with the reason on
  each.

### Other corrections

- An amount's adjustment needs a reason, names the balance it was made against, and is refused if
  the amount moved since.
- A move to another storage position needs a reason.
- A registrar's change to a fact needs a reason, and is a revision of the entry. A move of a record
  to the entry that superseded its own needs a reason, and repoints the project's references to the
  old entry, each a revision with that reason.
- Reviewing a hold needs a reason, and a person: not an agent or a service account, and not whoever
  saved the record.
