## In short

Some types say what makes two records the same thing: a compound by its structure, a strain by its
accession. Such a type is **registered**. Each identity has one **registry entry**, with one ID,
such as `CPD-000087`, and the values that belong to the identity. The entry belongs to no project.

Each project that has the thing keeps its own **record** of the entry, with the type's other fields,
which it revises and archives on its own. The record shares the entry's ID. Registering something
that's registered already gives the project a record of the existing entry, so one thing has one ID
however many projects use it.

## How it works

### An entry and its records

An entry holds three kinds of value:

- **Identity fields**, which make it this one. They're given when it's registered, and never
  change.
- **Derived values**, which follow from the identity alone, such as a compound's formula. They're
  set once, by whoever registers it or by the action that computes them.
- **Registry facts**, which are curated rather than computed, such as a CAS number. Anyone who
  holds the entry may set one while it's empty. After that only the registry's registrars change
  it, with a reason.

Every other field is the record's: a name the project uses, its notes, a LIMS ID. Two projects'
records of one entry can say different things in those fields.

A project has at most one record of each entry. A type with no identity has no entries: a lot, a
sample or a tube is its project's record alone, and refers to the entry of what it's a lot of.

### The registry is the boundary of identity

Each project is in one registry. A deployment has a default registry, and Org Admins add others for
programs that must stay apart, such as a contract lab's clients. Identities are compared only
within a registry, and a record can refer only to entries in its own registry.

### Registering

Creating a record of a registered type looks its identity up in the project's registry:

- If it isn't there, a new entry is registered, with a new ID, and the project gets its record.
- If it's there and the project has no record of it, the project gets a new record of that entry,
  with only the values it gave.
- If the project has a record of it already, that record is returned unchanged.

The second case tells the person registering that the thing exists, and its ID. It never tells them
who registered it, when, which projects hold it, or what any of them recorded.

### Who sees an entry

An entry is visible only through a record of it. Its ID alone grants nothing: someone whose
projects hold no record of `CPD-000087` gets "not found" for it, as for anything they can't read.
Holders see the entry's values, and in its history, what registrars changed, who and why, and what
was set through the projects they can read. Of anything else, they see only what was set.

### Registrars

Org Admins make people **registrars** of a registry. A registrar curates its facts, and supersedes
an entry registered in error with the right one. They see every entry in the registry and how many
projects hold each, never which projects, nor their records. Being an Org Admin doesn't make
someone a registrar.

## Worked example

The deployment's default registry holds the projects **Formulation** and **Stability**.

On 3 November 2026 Marta Okafor, in Formulation, registers Tris base from its structure, with the
small molecule plugin. Nothing in the registry has that identity, so it's a new entry, `CPD-000087`,
and Formulation gets its record. The plugin's trigger, which Formulation's Admins turned on, runs
once for the new entry and fills in its formula, C4H11NO3, and its molecular weight, 121.14 g/mol.
Marta adds its CAS number, 77-86-1, while the entry has none.

On 10 November Luis Romero, in Stability, registers Tris base too, from the same structure. Stability
gets its own record of `CPD-000087`. Luis learns that it's registered, and its ID; not that
Formulation holds it, nor who registered it.

| Field            | Whose         | In Formulation's record | In Stability's record |
| ---------------- | ------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------- |
| Structure        | The entry's   | Tris base's             | The same              |
| Molecular weight | The entry's   | 121.14 g/mol            | The same              |
| CAS number       | The entry's   | 77-86-1                 | The same              |
| Name             | Each record's | Tris base               | Trometamol            |
| Synonyms         | Each record's | Tris, THAM              | None                  |

Luis's lot of it, `GLOT-000015`, refers to `CPD-000087`: the reference holds the entry, so it reads
the same in every project that can see it. When Luis renames Stability's record, Formulation's
record doesn't change. Luis can't change the CAS number now that it's set: only a registrar can,
saying why, and both projects' holders are told.

## The rules in full

### Entries and records

- A registry entry has a registry and no project. A record has a project. The database holds each
  of these, and that an identity is registered once per registry and held once per project.
- An entry's ID is its type's prefix and a number, never given to another entry or record.
- A record of an entry is addressed by its own UUID, or by the entry's ID and its project. Without a
  project, the ID names the one record the reader can read; a reader of several is asked which.
- References to a registered type hold the entry. A project refers only to entries it holds a record
  of, its own or one shared into it: "refers to `CPD-000087`, which isn't in Stability: add it to
  the project first, by registering it there."
- Fields of an entry refer only to other entries.
- A type with an identity has no amount, and no states on its entries: lots, samples and tubes are
  records of their own, with their own amounts and states. A lock freezes a record's own values,
  not its entry's.

### Values

- Identity fields never change once they're set, and every identity has at least one required
  field. Iki compares identities by a hash of their values, read as what they are rather than as
  they were typed.
- A registration of an existing entry that gives a derived value it hasn't got fills it in. A
  derived value that differs from the entry's is the entry's already, and the entry's stays.
- A registration or a run that gives a fact the entry hasn't got fills it in. One that gives a fact
  that differs is refused, without saying what the entry holds.
- A registrar changes a fact with a reason, as a revision of the entry of kind **Curated**, audited
  and told to every project holding it.
- A fixed fact is given once, and then changed by no one, registrars included. A wrong one is
  corrected by superseding its entry.
- A fact is never free text: it's a number, a date, a choice, a reference to another entry, or text
  with a pattern or a format.

### Superseding and moving

- A registrar supersedes an entry with another live entry of the same type in the same registry,
  with a reason. It can't be undone. The superseded entry keeps its ID and values, says what
  replaced it, and can't be registered again.
- Each holding project's Admins, and whoever added its record, are told.
- An Editor of a holding project moves its record to the replacement, with a reason. The record
  keeps its data, history and runs, and the project's references to the old entry are pointed at
  the new one, each a revision with that reason. Archived records, and records a state locks,
  keep their references to the old entry.
- A record that's archived, or that a state locks, can't be moved until it's restored or unlocked.
- A project that holds the replacement already archives one of its two records instead.

### Projects and registries

- A project's registry is chosen when it's made, from those its team may use. An Org Admin can
  change it until the project has records; after that it's fixed.
- Adding an entry to another project gives that project a new, empty record of its own: the
  identity goes, none of the first project's data. Linking a record shares its data, as the first
  project's sharing setting allows.
- Runs register what they create in their project's registry, under the same rules as a person.
