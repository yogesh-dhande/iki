<Layer n={1} id="short" title="In short">
  <p class="lead">A plasmid prep is a tube of material made from a registered plasmid. Its <strong>verification</strong> says whether the tube holds that plasmid: Iki compares the prep's sequencing with the plasmid's own registered sequence, and a reviewer records the outcome and signs it.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>The comparison is evidence: computed once and never edited.</li>
    <li>The outcome is a decision: a match, a discrepancy or inconclusive, with notes and signatures.</li>
    <li>Releasing the lot is a separate decision again.</li>
  </ul>
</Layer>

<Layer n={4} id="rules" title="The rules in full">
<Rules>
<Rule id="pins" summary="What a verification pins" open>

One physical prep, its plasmid's registry entry, and the project plasmid record and revision
examined. The revision comes from the prep's pinned reference in the run's input, so a later edit
to the plasmid can't change what was checked. The project record tells apart two projects'
records of one registry entry.

The evidence is an alignment of the prep's reads, an externally reviewed report, or both. An
alignment's reference must be the plasmid's own registered DNA sequence, and the verification
keeps the alignment's revision; the alignment keeps the run and the read files it came from. For
an external report, the reviewer attests that it belongs to this prep and this reference.

</Rule>
<Rule id="agreement" summary="Matches, differs, incomplete">

The alignment's agreement is computed, one of three values:

- **matches**: every base is called and agrees with the record.
- **differs**: at least one difference.
- **incomplete**: nothing differs, but some bases aren't called.

A base the record's IUPAC code allows, and an N in the consensus, are not differences.

The verification's outcome is the reviewer's: **match**, **discrepancy** or **inconclusive**. A
clean result supports a match. Differences support a discrepancy, or a match with notes explaining
why each doesn't count against the prep. Recording a match over an alignment that differs or is
incomplete needs a written reason, which is part of what the signatures attest.

</Rule>
<Rule id="naming" summary="How differences are named (HGVS, o. and g.)">

Each difference is named in HGVS (den Dunnen et al. 2016): `o.` on a circle, `g.` on a linear
molecule. Positions are 1-based, with insertions and deletions moved to their 3'-most position. The
kinds are substitution, deletion, duplication, insertion, inversion and deletion-insertion; for
example `o.887del`, one base deleted at 887.

Each difference also records the plasmid's features it falls in, from the sequence's own features
and the plasmid's annotation, kept as they were when compared; the length of the run of one base it
lies in; and, from reads, its depth, the reads that agree with it and how many on each strand.

</Rule>
<Rule id="circle" summary="Reads round a circle, and homopolymer errors">

A plasmid is a circle, so a read can cross the origin, and so can a difference. Each read is
aligned with minimap2 to the plasmid's sequence doubled, so a read across the origin aligns in one
piece. Its insertions and deletions are moved 3' (Tan et al. 2015), so a deletion in a run of one
base counts at one position.

The consensus calls the majority base, or insertion, at each position with at least the minimum
depth of reads ({v.min_depth} here, the default); a position with fewer is N. It is then polished:
the reads are aligned to the consensus again until it stops changing, in at most four rounds of
alignment ({v.polishing_rounds} here). The consensus is compared with the record tripled, so it is
found whatever its rotation or strand.

Nanopore reads' errors cluster in runs of one base. Iki ships no basecaller and no neural polisher,
so a homopolymer error at modest depth can survive a majority call; the run length on each
difference is how a reviewer judges it. A service's own polished consensus can be compared instead.

</Rule>
<Rule id="corrections" summary="Corrections go back for review">

A correction to the outcome, the notes or the reviewer returns the verification to Recorded for a
new review, and keeps the earlier signatures in its history. New evidence is another verification
of the same prep, so a prep can hold several, each with its own reference revision and decision.

In Regulated QC the reviewer can't be the verification's creator, the analyst who submitted it, or
anyone who edited it, and QA must be independent of all of them and of the reviewer.

</Rule>
</Rules>
